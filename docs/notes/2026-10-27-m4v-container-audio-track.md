# The movie's own audio track, container half: nj_mp4 selects and iterates AMR-NB

The Player had one track per movie: the 3GP container reader (`nj_mp4`) picked a
video track and had no way to reach the AMR-NB sound track that the three
corpus movies carry next to it.  This round lands the container half of "a video
Player that also plays the movie's audio": selecting the sound track and handing
out its samples.  Commit `9e89681`.

The media-layer half (opening both tracks, decoding AMR and pushing PCM into the
same Player) is in progress on top of this API; nothing in this note claims it
is done.

## API

`src/midp/nj_mp4.h`:

    #define NJ_MP4_TRACK_VIDEO 0
    #define NJ_MP4_TRACK_AUDIO 1
    #define NJ_MP4_CODEC_SAMR 0x73616d72u   /* 'samr' */

    int nj_mp4_open_track(nj_mp4 *, const void *, nj_u4, int kind);
    int nj_mp4_open_audio(nj_mp4 *, const void *, nj_u4);
    int nj_mp4_open(...);      /* unchanged = open_track(..., TRACK_VIDEO) */

On success the iterator is filled like it is for a video track: `timescale` is
the track's `mdhd` timescale, `duration` its duration in that timescale,
`sample_count`, and `nj_mp4_next()` hands out `offset` (absolute in the caller's
buffer), `size`, `dts`, `duration` and `key`.  For audio the parser also fills
`channels` and `sample_rate` from the AudioSampleEntry, and `width`/`height`/
`fps` stay zero.  `nj_mp4_rewind()` restarts the iteration.

The selection distinguishes the cases a caller has to tell apart:

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `NJ_MP4_OK` | a `samr` sound track was found and its tables cross-check |
| `NJ_MP4_ERR_NO_AUDIO` (-5) | the file has no sound track at all |
| `NJ_MP4_ERR_AUDIO_CODEC` (-6) | the file has a sound track, but not AMR-NB |
| `NJ_MP4_ERR_FORMAT` (-1) | damaged container or tables that disagree |

`nj_mp4_open()`'s video selection - H.263 preferred over `mp4v`, its error codes,
and therefore the H.263/mp4v providers - is untouched; the shared table checks
run for both kinds, and the parser is still allocation-free with no globals.

The parser change is a deferred `stsd`: the box walk reads the sample-entry
fourcc, but the fixed part is laid out after the trak's `hdlr` is known
(78-byte VisualSampleEntry vs 28-byte AudioSampleEntry), because `hdlr` may
legally follow `minf`.  AMR-NB needs no decoder configuration from the
container: each AMR frame is self-describing, so neither `esds` nor `damr` is
read.

## The three corpus movies

Verified with `tools/mp4_audio_stats.c`, which parses with the same code the
Player will use; ffprobe agrees on sample counts and durations.

    cc -O2 -std=c99 -I include -I src/midp -o /tmp/mp4_audio_stats \
       tools/mp4_audio_stats.c src/midp/nj_mp4.c
    /tmp/mp4_audio_stats 1942.3gp doom.3gp movie.3gp

| Movie | codec | timescale | duration | samples | ChannelCount | rate | first sample | total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `1942.3gp` | `samr` | 8000 | 87200 (10.880 s) | 109 | 2 | 8000 | off 3703, size 160 | 17440 B |
| `vid/doom.3gp` | `samr` | 8000 | 99840 (12.480 s) | 624 | 1 | 8000 | off 28, size 32 | 19968 B |
| `movie.3gp` (HAWX) | `samr` | 8000 | 137600 (17.200 s) | 172 | 2 | 8000 | off 40, size 135 | 23220 B |

Two of the files declare `ChannelCount = 2` although AMR-NB is mono by
definition (ffprobe normalises it to 1).  The iterator reports what the file
says; AMR callers must treat the stream as mono.

**Sample granularity is not uniform**, and that matters to the audio decoder:

- `doom.3gp`: one sample is one 20 ms AMR frame (32 bytes, mode 7);
- `1942.3gp`: one sample is 100 ms, i.e. five 32-byte AMR frames (stts delta
  800 ticks);
- `movie.3gp`: one sample is 100 ms, five 27-byte AMR frames (mode 6).

The first sample of every file parses exactly into those frames with the AMR-NB
frame-size table (`{13,14,16,18,20,21,27,32}` bytes including the ToC byte for
FT 0..7, 6 for a SID frame, 1 for a no-data frame).  The container layer hands
the sample out as stored; splitting it into frames belongs to the audio path.

## Tests and evidence

`tests/m4v_audio_clip.h` (generated by `tools/gen_m4v_audio_clip.py`) is a real
1025-byte dual-track 3GP: a 64x48 `mp4v` track with two samples and a `samr`
track with five samples in two chunks (3 + 2, stts delta 160).  `test_m4v`
grows to 126 checks and covers:

- audio selection and its reported fields;
- all five samples' size, contiguous offset, dts, duration and key, end of
  track and rewind;
- the video track of the same buffer still selecting and iterating exactly as
  before (including its H.263/mp4v behaviour through the untouched open path);
- `nj_mp4_open_track()` by kind, and refusal of an unknown kind or NULL input;
- a video-only file (`NO_AUDIO`), a rewritten `mp4a` entry (`AUDIO_CODEC`), a
  sound track whose `stsz` count contradicts `stts` (`FORMAT`) and a truncated
  file (`FORMAT`).

Evidence: `.local-tools/debug-runs/m4v-audio-20261027/` - the three movies, the
tool's per-sample walk (`tool-stats.txt`), ffprobe's view (`ffprobe.txt`), the
synthetic fixture and a README with the table above.

The other suites stay green (test_h263 97, test_avcodec 102, test_lib 920),
test_m4v passes ASan/UBSan and `tools/check_no_statics.sh` still reports no
writable statics.
